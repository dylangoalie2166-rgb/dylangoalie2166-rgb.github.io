User Agreement

Version 2026-10-07 · Effective October 7, 2026

This is the agreement between you and BuckBack. It covers what BuckBack does for you, what you promise in return, what it costs, and what happens if something goes wrong. Please read it. You accept it by ticking the box and tapping "I agree" in the app, and we keep a record of the version you accepted and when.

BuckBack is operated by Dylan Karan in British Columbia, Canada ("BuckBack", "we", "us"). "You" means the person creating the account and the business you're creating it for.

1. Who can use BuckBack

  • You must be at least 19 years old and have the authority to agree to this for your business.
  • BuckBack is for businesses collecting money owed to them for goods or services they provided. It isn't for personal loans, for debts you've bought from someone else, or for collecting on behalf of anyone other than your own business.
  • You're responsible for everything done through your account. Keep your password to yourself.

2. What BuckBack does

  • You add a client and what they owe. BuckBack's AI assistant then phones and texts that client, in your business's name, to ask for payment, agree on a date, send a secure pay link and follow up. If you choose, it also emails a demand letter from your business.
  • By adding a client you are asking BuckBack to contact that client for you, about that balance, in those ways.
  • The assistant always says it is an AI assistant calling from your business. It calls only at the times shown in the app, and it stops calling and texting a client who clearly asks it to.
  • We don't promise that any client will pay. BuckBack is a tool for asking; whether a client pays is up to them.
  • BuckBack is not a law firm and doesn't give legal advice. Nothing in the app, a demand letter, or anything the in-app assistant tells you is legal advice. If you need it, talk to a lawyer.

3. What you promise

Every time you add a client, you're telling us all of the following is true:

  • The money is really owed to your business, by that client, for goods or services you actually provided, and it is already overdue.
  • The amount, the client's name and their contact details are correct to the best of your knowledge.
  • You're legally allowed to collect it. For example, it hasn't already been paid, isn't covered by a bankruptcy or consumer proposal, isn't barred by a court order, and isn't too old to be collected under the law.
  • You're allowed to give us that client's details and to have us contact them for you.
  • You'll keep your own records (your invoice or agreement, and proof you did the work), since you may need them if the client disputes the balance.

You also agree to:

  • Record payments honestly and promptly. If a client pays you directly (cash, cheque, e-transfer or anything else), record it in the app.
  • Answer truthfully when the app asks whether a client has paid.
  • Not use BuckBack to harass, threaten or mislead anyone, or to chase money you know isn't owed.
  • Follow the laws that apply to your business and to how you collect what you're owed.

4. The AI can make mistakes

The assistant listens, speaks and writes using AI. It can mishear a client, misunderstand what they meant, or summarise a call imperfectly. The app shows you what happened on each account; you're responsible for checking anything important before you act on it, and for telling us if something looks wrong. The in-app assistant's answers are for your information only. It can save notes you give it about a client, and it can offer to do things for you, but those only happen when you tap the button it shows and confirm; you're responsible for what you confirm.

Notes you add about a client are for the AI to use when it contacts them. Only add what's true and relevant to getting paid.

5. Demand letters

  • A demand letter is sent in your business's name, to your client, by email. It states the amount, what it's for, the date of the invoice or work, and the contact there has been, taken from what you entered and from that account's history.
  • The statements in the letter are yours. You're responsible for the amount, what it's for and the dates being accurate.
  • When you confirm that you're prepared to file a claim, the letter says your business intends to. Only confirm it if it's true.
  • Letters can be sent automatically (after 5 calls with no answer, or when a client asks the assistant to stop calling). That's on when you start; you can turn it off in Account.
  • Each client gets one letter. We can't recall an email once it's sent.

6. Getting paid

  • Clients pay through a secure page run by Stripe. The money goes from your client to your own Stripe account and then to your bank. BuckBack never holds your money.
  • To be paid this way you need a Stripe account, and Stripe's own terms and fees apply to it.
  • Refunds, chargebacks and disputes over a payment are between you, your client and Stripe.

7. BuckBack's fee

  • No monthly fee. BuckBack keeps a share of what's recovered on each account: 5% of the first $10,000, 3% from there up to $50,000, and 1.5% above that. The fee on a payment is at least $2 and never more than 20% of that payment. Amounts are in Canadian dollars, plus any tax that applies.
  • Paid through the pay link: the fee is taken from the payment automatically.
  • Paid to you directly, after BuckBack has contacted the client: the same fee applies. It goes on a monthly statement, charged to the card or bank account you saved for fees.
  • If BuckBack never contacted the client before they paid, there's no fee.
  • If a client tells the assistant they've already paid, the app asks you to confirm or dispute it. If you don't answer within 7 days, it's treated as paid and the fee applies.
  • The amount owed can't be changed after a client is added. The app asks you to confirm it first.
  • Authorisation to charge. By saving a card or bank account for fees, you authorise BuckBack to charge it, through Stripe, for the fees on each monthly statement. If a charge fails and stays unpaid for a week, the assistant pauses until it's settled.
  • If you delete your account, any fees not yet billed are charged first.
  • Stripe's own processing fee on a link payment is separate and goes to Stripe.

8. Not working around the fee

The fee is how BuckBack is paid for the work it does. You agree not to avoid it, for example by taking a payment from a client BuckBack has contacted and not recording it, by closing an account as unpaid when it was paid, or by saying a client hasn't paid when they have. When an account BuckBack has worked is closed without a payment, the client is told and can tell us if they paid. If we find a fee was avoided, we may charge the fee that was owed and suspend or close your account.

9. Privacy

Our Privacy Policy explains what information BuckBack handles and who it's shared with. You're responsible for having the right to share your clients' information with us. Calls are not recorded as audio.

10. Ending this agreement

  • You can stop at any time by deleting your account in the app (Account → Delete account). That stops all calls and texts and permanently removes your data.
  • We may pause or close your account if you break this agreement, if fees go unpaid, if we receive complaints about how the service is being used, or if we believe an account is being used unlawfully or to collect money that isn't owed.
  • We may also stop offering BuckBack. If we do, we'll give you reasonable notice where we can.
  • Fees already earned are still owed after the agreement ends.

BuckBack is provided as it is. We work to keep it running and accurate, but we don't promise that it will be uninterrupted or error-free, that every call or text will connect or be delivered, that an email will reach an inbox rather than a junk folder, or that any amount will be recovered. BuckBack relies on other companies' services (phone, AI, payments, email); we aren't responsible for their outages.

12. Limit on our liability

  • To the extent the law allows, BuckBack's total liability to you for anything connected with the service is limited to the fees you paid BuckBack in the 12 months before the claim arose, or CA$100, whichever is more.
  • To the extent the law allows, BuckBack isn't liable for lost profits, lost business, lost clients, or other indirect or consequential losses.
  • Nothing here limits liability that can't legally be limited.

13. Your responsibility for claims

If someone brings a claim against BuckBack because of something you did, for example because you added money that wasn't owed, gave us wrong information, had no right to share a client's details, or broke this agreement or the law, you agree to cover BuckBack's reasonable costs and losses from that claim.

14. Changes to this agreement

If we change this agreement, we'll post the new version here with a new date. For changes that matter, the app will show you the new version and ask you to accept it before you continue. A change to the fee will be announced at least 30 days ahead and won't apply to payments already made.
